\section{Causal Target and Identification Requirements}
\label{sec:causal}

\paragraph{Unit and admissibility.}
Let \(i\) index a source--target pair, not an execution seed.  Define
\(A_i=1\) when pair \(i\) satisfies the source, target, transfer, and
measurement requirements that can be established before assignment and before
any target outcome is observed.  Artifact-bound admissibility is therefore a
property of a prospectively locked pair and evaluation environment; it is not
retrofitted to successful runs.

\paragraph{Assignment and potential outcomes.}
For the primary intended contrast, \(T_i=1\) assigns the relevant,
source-correct and source-focal-safe procedural memory whose named
applicability condition fails at the target, while \(T_i=0\) assigns a
prospectively selected irrelevant correct memory under the same
post-ingestion capacity.  Let \(F_i(t)\in\{0,1\}\) denote genuine completion of
the requested task under assignment \(t\), and let \(S_i(t)\in\{0,1\}\) denote
success on the focal security property.  The joint adverse outcome is
\begin{equation}
  H_i(t)=F_i(t)\{1-S_i(t)\},
  \label{eq:joint-outcome}
\end{equation}
so \(H_i(t)=1\) only for a task-completing, focal-insecure result.  The primary
intended estimand is
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\mathrm{adm}}
  =\mathbb{E}\!\left[H_i(1)-H_i(0)\mid A_i=1\right].
  \label{eq:estimand}
\end{equation}
This is a target quantity, not a reported effect estimate: the completed work
does not estimate \(\tau_{\mathrm{adm}}\).

\paragraph{Assignment is not delivery or use.}
We distinguish assigned condition \(T_i\); verified memory delivery
\(D_i(t)\); usable context/action/runtime capacity \(C_i(t)\); observable
uptake \(U_i(t)\); applicability checking \(K_i(t)\); implementation
\(I_i(t)\); and the two outcomes \(F_i(t)\) and \(S_i(t)\).
Delivery is required for treatment fidelity but does not establish uptake.
Uptake and applicability checking occur after assignment and are process
measures or mediators.  They cannot be moved into \(A_i\), and an
uptake-stratified contrast would require a different causal target.
Likewise, \(F_i(t)\) is treatment-responsive.  Reporting security only among
observed task-completing runs can condition on a post-treatment variable and
change the estimand; Equation~\ref{eq:joint-outcome} instead defines the joint
outcome for every assigned unit.

\paragraph{Identification logic.}
The contract follows from the claim rather than from an unconstrained
checklist.  Because \(H\) is a conjunction, target/outcome validity must show
that the task is initially incomplete, that insecure and secure completions
are both attainable, that security repair retains the requested feature, and
that one focal witness distinguishes them.  Because the intervention is
described as transfer of a source-correct and source-safe procedure across an
applicability shift, source/transfer validity must establish correctness,
focal safety, operation-level relevance, a named predicate, its directional
source--target shift, and the absence of a second comparably material
mismatch.  Because \(\tau_{\mathrm{adm}}\) contrasts assigned memory content,
treatment validity must establish delivery fidelity, resource balance, the
semantic comparator, and outcome-blind source locking.  Finally, claims about
mechanism or uncertainty require delivery/uptake separation, a joint
task-security analysis, and defensible experimental units.

These requirements support a conventional randomized contrast only with
consistency, positivity within \(A_i=1\), no cross-unit interference, valid
outcome measurement, and assignment that is independent of potential outcomes
under the frozen design.  In V1, assignment changed both semantic information
and usable capacity, so \(C_i(1)\neq C_i(0)\) by design.  Even with otherwise
valid endpoints, that assignment would identify a bundled intervention rather
than a semantic-memory effect.
