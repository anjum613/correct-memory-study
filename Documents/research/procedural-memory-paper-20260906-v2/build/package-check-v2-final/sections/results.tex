\paragraph{Correct memory has no established common harmful effect.}
Table~\ref{tab:contrasts} gives the corrected comparisons. C$-$N changes $U$ by $\GPTFiveFiveCminusNU$, $\LunaCminusNU$, and $\TerraCminusNU$ percentage points for GPT-5.5, Luna, and Terra, and by $\QwenNextCminusNU$, $\QwenThirtyCminusNU$, and $\DevstralCminusNU$ for Qwen Next, Qwen 30B, and Devstral. The signs differ, and every C$-$N bootstrap interval includes zero. The original hypothesis is therefore not established across the evaluated configurations.

\paragraph{Joint outcomes distinguish improvement from non-completion.}
For Qwen 30B, C$-$N lowers $U$ by 11.5 points and functionality by 15.4 points. Across its thirteen eligible families, functional runs fall from 24 to 20 out of 26, and functionality and focal witness passes fall from 8 to 7. This is not an improvement that preserves functionality. Devstral's $U$ also falls, by 4.2 points, while functionality rises by 4.2 points. Identical endpoint directions can describe different changes in performance.

Figure~\ref{fig:joint} retains all recorded runs. Qwen Next has \QwenNextnotFS{} valid $\neg F\land S$ outcomes, all with unchanged final implementations in the saved audit. Luna has \LunanotFS{} such outcomes; sixteen have empty patches and visible complaints about unavailable named tools. These observed non-completions make a security pass rate alone misleading.

\paragraph{The reminder reduces observed failures in Codex.}
B$-$C lowers $U$ by 30.0, 13.6, and 22.7 points in the three Codex configurations. Functionality is unchanged for GPT-5.5 and Terra and rises by 4.5 points for Luna. MiniSWE estimates are 0.0, $+4.2$, and 0.0 points. The distinction concerns complete configurations, including their interfaces. None of the C$-$N or B$-$C exact family sign flip diagnostics is below .05. GPT-5.5's B$-$C bootstrap interval excludes zero, whereas its sign flip diagnostic is .125; few families have nonzero differences and the procedures make different assumptions.

\paragraph{The direction survives unresolved measurements in the recorded cohort.}
The 29 invalids comprise four interruptions, four timeouts, seventeen candidate execution exceptions, and four lowering incompatibilities. The seventeen exceptions are in Codex X28. All have completed functionality passes, while their security evaluation stops on an exception. The four timeouts retain both completed component checks: three pass functionality, one fails it, and all four pass the focal witness. The other eight invalids have neither component resolved. Thus 21 invalid runs retain functionality information and four retain security information; $U$ remains unresolved for 25.

Table~\ref{tab:bounds} uses this information without rescoring invalids. Even the largest admissible B$-$C bound is negative for each Codex configuration: $-23.1$, $-3.8$, and $-11.5$ points. Their descriptive direction therefore does not depend on discarding unresolved outcomes. The bounds use all thirteen families and need not contain a complete family estimate that uses a different family set. Appendix~\ref{app:missingness} reports treatment specific categories and the wider bounds obtained by discarding completed components.

\paragraph{Family variation and witness scope matter.}
The full family overview is in Appendix~\ref{app:missingness}. In F04, all six Codex B runs pass both checks, compared with six functional witness failures under N; all six MiniSWE B runs are functional witness failures. Such observed variation can be obscured by aggregation. Omitting X06 changes GPT-5.5 C$-$N from $+9.1$ to 0.0 points, while all three Codex B$-$C estimates remain negative. I$-$N ranges from $-8.3$ to $+7.7$ points. The irrelevant condition controls for a supplied memory envelope, but is not an inert placebo in every observed cell. Scope omissions and C$-$I are supplementary analyses, not replacement primary contrasts.
