# Annotator B methodology and ambiguity note

This is an independent **LLM annotation pass**, not human annotation. I manually coded all 52 blinded packets (P001–P052) under the supplied frozen codebook. I read the complete packet text, using sequential chunks for large files. I inspected only the codebook, those packets, and my own output for formatting and validation. I did not access model mappings, evaluator scores, private study files, or the other annotator’s output; I did not discuss case labels with the other annotator. No quoted command, candidate implementation, or test was executed, and no browsing or API calls were used. Python was used for reading text and formatting or structurally validating my manually reasoned labels, not as a keyword classifier.

For A, I required an explicit pre-edit statement connecting the relevant source assumption with the target change. Silence is coded as absence of that observable statement, not ignored memory. I retained U where a specific safeguard was described before editing but the source-assumption contrast remained semantically uncertain. Comments introduced by the first edit and later explanations do not retroactively establish pre-edit recognition. The first-edit field identifies the first relevant substantive implementation edit, excluding failed calls, no-op rewrites, temporary test files, and preparatory deletion during a subsequent replacement; individual notes explain material chronology issues.

P describes structural compatibility with the comparison source procedure and the presence or absence of the focal check. It does not establish copying, retrieval, or use of delivered memory, including when the packet supplies an unrelated memory. T records a concrete protection mechanism in the final implementation, independently of reported outcomes and overall contract correctness. Partial mechanisms can therefore receive T=Y while notes retain remaining defects. Runtime-enforced limits alone are not treated as a candidate-implemented protection. Z counts substantive but incorrect or syntactically unsupported implementations as meaningful attempts; an unchanged feature-disabled baseline remains Z=Y and normally P/T=NA.

The trace sometimes contains embedded truncation notices in tool responses; I read the supplied text without reconstructing unavailable content. Masking is practical rather than guaranteed because interfaces can suggest configuration. Separate annotation contexts prevent direct label coordination but do not remove possible shared model or training biases. Human annotation and validation have not been completed. Disagreements should be reported without using final scores to choose a consensus label.
