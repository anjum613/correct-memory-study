\title{Passing Without Solving:\\
An Identifiability Audit of Security-Sensitive Agent Memory}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
This paper does not estimate a causal harmful-memory effect.  We ask whether
assigning a relevant procedural memory that is demonstrably correct and
focal-safe in its source context changes task-complete insecurity when a named
security-relevant applicability condition fails at the target.  An evaluation
initially designed for that contrast did not identify it: its functionality
oracle accepted no-op states, focal security was at floor, memory delivery
changed usable agent resources, preserved trajectories showed no substantive
observable procedural uptake, and nominal seeds added almost no substantive
variation.  From the intended estimand we derive an identification contract
covering target/outcome, source/transfer, treatment, and process/analysis
validity.  A prospectively bounded, artifact-grounded real-repository
construction study admitted no complete pair before its frozen stop rule; this
is a feasibility result, not a population-frequency estimate.  A constructed
known-valid control traversed the unchanged contract and two targeted near
misses were rejected at their intended gates.  Applying the framework to six
deliberately selected close works yielded a partially distinctive framework:
the papers instantiate different legitimate subsets of evidence, while none as
published establishes the full contract for our stronger estimand.  Human
verification of that AI-assisted audit remains pending.
\end{abstract}
